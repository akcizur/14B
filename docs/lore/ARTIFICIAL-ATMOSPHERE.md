# Artificial Atmosphere, Sky & Illumination

## Status

**CANON:** District 14 has an artificial atmosphere inside a protective space-habitat system.

**OPEN:** Atmospheric composition, pressure, circulation, environmental control mechanism, sky appearance, lighting source, weather, day/night timing, and visibility parameters have not been determined.

## Atmosphere goals

The artificial atmosphere should make the city feel habitable and spatially deep without looking like a simple Earth environment pasted inside a space enclosure.

The visual system should support:
- distance haze and atmospheric perspective;
- localized volumetric light where performance allows;
- readable silhouettes at long street distances;
- consistent outdoor and indoor light relationships;
- distinct but subtle transitions between street, roof, and aerial layers;
- intentional visibility of the protective ring and outer-space views.

## Sky and outer-space visibility

The sky is a designed habitat environment. It may combine a simulated sky, controlled luminous surfaces, distant artificial structures, astronomical background elements, or a blend of these. The exact mechanism is OPEN.

Do not make stars visible uniformly through all views if the habitat's enclosure should occlude them. The sky, ring, and space backdrop must agree with the chosen ring geometry and sightline.

## Lighting

The light source and day/night cycle are OPEN. Before implementation is finalized, define:
- whether lighting is primarily simulated natural light, artificial habitat lighting, or hybrid;
- direction, color temperature, intensity, and shadow behavior;
- cycle duration and phases, if a cycle exists;
- street and building supplemental lighting;
- emergency/maintenance lighting;
- how ring structure illumination affects the city.

Avoid permanent oversaturation, heavy bloom, or a generic neon wash. The architecture should remain readable and retain its Central-European material character.

## Sound and environmental cues (future)

Atmosphere can be communicated through subtle ventilation ambience, distant structural hum, wind-like circulation, changes in noise near service infrastructure, and occlusion between street canyons. Soundscape details remain future design work and must not imply an exact technical system before one is established.
