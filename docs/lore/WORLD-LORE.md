# District 14 — World Lore

## 1. Core premise

District 14 is a deliberately bounded urban habitat in outer space. Its inhabited city-space is protected by a gigantic ring structure referred to in the project concept as the **Starship Ring**. The ring is a defining feature of the setting: a protective boundary, an immense piece of infrastructure, and a visual landmark whose scale should be felt from within the city.

District 14 is not a conventional open-world city. Its designed urban footprint is intentionally limited to **three streets**. The Main Street is the dominant, unusually long urban axis. The other two streets are secondary and exist to give the place depth, connection, and local variation without creating a sprawling road grid.

The city grows vertically as well as longitudinally. Roofs, elevated transport, aerial corridors, technical structures, and upper-level connections can expand the usable world above the ground street.

## 2. Setting pillars

1. **Artificial, not planetary:** the world is an engineered habitat in space, not a natural city on a planet.
2. **Protected urban life:** the ring and habitat systems create a controlled living environment.
3. **Three streets only:** the street count is a deliberate world constraint, not a temporary generator setting.
4. **A long Main Street:** the central street is a key navigational and visual spine and should appear to extend far beyond the immediate playable segment.
5. **Vertical urbanity:** the world may extend upward through buildings, roofs, transport infrastructure, and aerial space rather than outward through endless blocks.
6. **Czechoslovak architectural memory:** the city draws strongly from architecture and urban character of 1950–1975 Czechoslovakia.
7. **Retro-future technology:** futuristic systems are present, but should feel integrated, aged, maintained, and functional instead of uniformly glossy neon.
8. **Artificial atmosphere and sky:** sky, haze, illumination, and atmospheric depth are designed environmental systems, not a default Earth skybox.

## 3. Spatial identity

The setting should feel compact when described on a map but expansive when experienced from street level. Long sightlines, scale cues, atmospheric depth, partial ring visibility, building rhythm, and vertical layers create the illusion of a much larger place.

The three streets must have a clear hierarchy. The Main Street is the unmistakable spine; the secondary streets are subordinate and should not accidentally read as competing long avenues.

The streets do not need to be symmetrical. Their exact geometry and connections remain open design questions until a masterplan is approved.

## 4. Tone and mood

District 14 should feel:

- lived-in, quiet, strange, and subtly isolated;
- familiar through Central-European residential forms;
- futuristic through infrastructure, transport, materials, and environmental systems;
- spatially immense, while the actual playable street network remains controlled;
- atmospheric without being perpetually dark, noisy, or saturated with neon.

The contrast between ordinary everyday urban life and the fact that the entire city is enclosed in a space habitat is a defining narrative tension. The setting does not need to constantly explain this contrast. It can be communicated through views, sounds, infrastructure, signage, maintenance details, and occasional glimpses of the ring and outer space.

## 5. Narrative restraint

World lore should not be delivered only through exposition. The player can discover setting context from environmental storytelling: public notices, building labels, transit signage, service doors, technical equipment, architecture, distant views, and local routines.

The exact origin, purpose, founding era, population, political system, and events of District 14 are intentionally unspecified at this stage. Do not invent definitive answers in implementation or marketing copy without recording and approving them.

## 6. Canon and uncertainty

A decision is canon only after it is clearly accepted and documented. Prototype meshes, temporary heights, guessed dimensions, generated reference imagery, and untested assumptions are not canon.

Use labels consistently:
- **CANON** — approved setting decision.
- **PROVISIONAL** — current proposal awaiting review.
- **OPEN** — not decided.
- **REJECTED** — considered and intentionally not used, with rationale when useful.

## 7. Current canon summary

- Artificial space habitat in outer space.
- Protected by a massive ring structure called the Starship Ring in the current concept.
- Artificial atmosphere.
- Exactly three streets.
- A long, dominant Main Street.
- Vertical expansion and aerial transport are part of the concept.
- Czechoslovak 1950–1975 urban/architectural character with restrained retro-futurist technology.
