# Masterplan Study B — proportions, cross-section, and spatial scale

**Status:** Proposed study only. No values are canon or implementation-ready.
**Purpose:** Establish coherent proportions for the habitat, street hierarchy, buildings, vertical city, and aerial clearance before building geometry.

## 1. Design goal

District 14 should feel like a real, inhabited urban space, not a narrow sci-fi tunnel or an endless procedural city. The main street must be long enough to establish a horizon and urban continuity, with buildings on both sides. The enclosure should feel large enough that its curvature and structural elements are not constantly dominating the street-level view.

Design in three scales:
1. **Habitat scale:** outer envelope, width, height, and structural character.
2. **District scale:** exactly three streets and the Main Street character sequence.
3. **Playable slice:** a deliberately limited portion of the world, built and verified to a high standard.

## 2. Three proportional envelope studies

The dimensions below are scenario ranges for comparing scale, not engineering facts or final world dimensions. Length is measured along Main Street.

| Parameter | Compact study | Medium study (recommended to test first) | Large study |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Habitat length | 1.2–1.8 km | 2.0–3.2 km | 4–6 km |
| Main Street continuous urban axis | 0.7–1.2 km | 1.2–2.0 km | 2.5–4 km |
| Habitat usable width | 250–400 m | 450–700 m | 800–1,200 m |
| Central street-zone width (façade to façade, including road and sidewalks) | 25–45 m | 35–65 m | 50–90 m |
| Typical residential height | 12–28 m | 15–40 m | 18–55 m |
| Rare landmark height | 35–60 m | 45–90 m | 70–140 m |

These values should not be interpreted as exact ratios to be enforced. Width and height must be checked against the enclosure cross-section and visual perception. The “large” option may be wasteful for the game's actual scope; the “compact” option risks revealing the enclosure too often.

## 3. Recommended first test: medium envelope

**Recommendation:** test the medium scenario as the initial visual blockout. It offers enough room for a long street, varied skyline, short side branches, distant views, and vertical mobility while keeping the game world manageable.

A rough planning target (not approved):
- habitat envelope: approximately **2.6 km long**;
- Main Street: approximately **1.5 km long**;
- urban usable width: approximately **550 m**;
- Main Street façade-to-façade zone: approximately **45 m**;
- standard residential buildings: **4–8 storeys** with a restrained variation in heights;
- rare taller landmark: only after the overall section and skyline have been tested.

Do not build the whole length as a fully detailed continuous street. The world can be a long coherent masterplan while gameplay and asset detail are focused on a smaller verified slice, with distant areas simplified and/or streamed later.

## 4. Main Street cross-section: layers

The first cross-section should use relative proportions and be refined against the reference building.

From one façade to the other, the street is composed of:

1. Building edge and entrance/setback zone.
2. Continuous pedestrian sidewalk.
3. Curb/edge buffer.
4. Ground vehicle roadway.
5. Opposite-side buffer and sidewalk.
6. Entrance/setback and façade edge.

Do not make the roadway so wide that the pedestrian street loses intimacy, or so narrow that vehicle turning, collision, or passing becomes impossible. Ground traffic and aerial traffic must have separate clearance envelopes. The street must remain pedestrian-readable even with vehicles present.

### Main Street width comparison

- **Narrow / intimate:** 25–35 m façade-to-façade. Residential scale, tighter street canyon, less room for aerial traffic close to street.
- **Balanced:** 35–55 m. Recommended first blockout range. Allows sidewalks, street furniture, vehicle zone, and façade variation.
- **Broad / monumental:** 55–90 m. Suitable for a special node or landmark, not necessarily the entire street. Risks reducing ordinary housing to a distant wall.

Do not choose a final width until the reference building, sidewalks, and vehicle dimensions are placed together and tested at human eye height.

## 5. Three streets: spatial roles and relative lengths

Keep exactly three named streets. The branches must not read as a grid or create new parallel streets.

Suggested relative lengths (proposal):
- **Street 01 / Main Street:** 100% of the principal urban axis.
- **Street 02:** about 8–18% of Main Street length.
- **Street 03:** about 12–25% of Main Street length, with a different endpoint and visual role.

These ratios are only for planning. The secondary streets should be physically short branches, not miniature versions of Main Street. They can vary in width and direction, and one may widen into a local node. Avoid both branches having identical length, shape, building rhythm, and endpoint.

### Branch placement

Study a staggered arrangement:
- Street 02 branches from Main Street at an earlier point, on one side.
- Street 03 branches farther along, on the opposite side.
- They are not aligned across the Main Street and are not parallel.
- Their endpoints are intentionally different: a local space, courtyard-like widening, or service destination are candidates, not decisions.

## 6. Longitudinal rhythm of Main Street

Use zones to vary the street, but do not turn zones into obvious repeated modules.

A conceptual sequence:
- **A — Arrival / ordinary residential:** simple housing façades, entrances, sidewalk life; enclosure not yet obvious.
- **B — Neighborhood activity:** a denser service/local node, widened sidewalk or small gathering space.
- **C — Quiet residential:** lower visual noise and more consistent housing rhythm.
- **D — Landmark / intersection:** unique silhouette or structural event, potentially at Street 03.
- **E — Continuing urban edge:** the street remains visually continuous beyond the playable area, with distance fog and simplified silhouettes.

The order is flexible. Do not lock a zone to a specific kilometer mark. Do not use hard segment cuts; use gradual transitions in façade rhythm, height, lighting, and space.

## 7. Building height and skyline proportions

Treat the existing reference house as the baseline building family, but it does not define every building. First test a small family:

- **Low buildings:** 2–4 storeys, create breathing space and skyline breaks.
- **Standard housing:** 4–8 storeys, dominant everyday residential type.
- **Tall residential:** 9–14 storeys, uncommon and clustered only where the masterplan supports it.
- **Landmark:** exceptional height and silhouette, not a repeated asset.

The exact floor count, floor heights, and building footprints must be measured and documented for each approved prototype. Do not add arbitrary extra storeys to stretch the skyline. Avoid continuous high-rise canyon walls.

## 8. Vertical city and airspace

The vertical city should feel integrated with housing and infrastructure, not layered like a stacked sci-fi highway.

Candidate vertical elements:
- stair and lift cores that logically connect levels;
- roof access on selected buildings;
- maintenance walkways and limited roof bridges;
- occasional elevated service infrastructure;
- aerial vehicles occupying a defined clearance layer above the roofs.

Aerial travel is a distinct traffic system. It must have defined height bands, approach/landing rules, and collision clearances after the street/building heights are agreed. The previous provisional air corridor values are not used in this study.

## 9. Habitat cross-section: avoid the tunnel trap

The enclosure cross-section must not be designed as a constant smooth tube with a bright ceiling directly above Main Street.

Study:
- a broad central urban void with the enclosure rising well beyond the standard roofline;
- asymmetric enclosure walls and structure, so the habitat is not a perfect symmetrical tunnel;
- distant structural elements that fade into haze;
- selected roof or aerial vantage points where the curvature is revealed;
- visually calm ceiling/sky treatment at street level, with structure concentrated in selected areas.

No final cross-section or gravity mechanism is selected. The enclosure dimensions must be compatible with its fictional pressure, radiation protection, and atmospheric function, but these functions remain worldbuilding rather than verified engineering.

## 10. Scale checks to perform before geometry approval

Create a simple blockout only after the concept is approved, then check:

- Can a player see at least several blocks ahead without the street feeling artificially straight or empty?
- Do façades on both sides read as individual buildings, rather than one repeating wall?
- Can the habitat enclosure be kept out of the dominant eye-level view in ordinary street areas, yet revealed from selected locations?
- Is a 4–8 storey housing band plausible within the proposed cross-section?
- Does a broad street still feel inhabited and walkable?
- Can a hover vehicle pass above roofs without intersecting the ring structure or feeling too close to pedestrians?
- Do the three streets remain visually and spatially distinct?
- Can a playable slice be detailed without having to finish kilometers of city?

## 11. First playable slice — recommended scope

**Do not start with the entire Main Street.** Start with a contiguous, fully measured slice around the first intersection or neighborhood node.

Suggested test scope (proposal):
- 120–220 m of Main Street;
- one complete intersection with Street 02 or Street 03;
- 3–6 building footprints, including one approved reference building and one contrasting type;
- one coherent pedestrian route;
- street/sidewalk/curb proportions;
- roofline and aerial clearance blockout;
- one view that hints at the enclosure;
- no furniture or detailed façade decoration until the spatial test is approved.

This test slice is an architectural proof of scale, not a final game level. Extend it only when it works at walking speed, in the vehicle, and from a roof/aerial viewpoint.

## 13. Next gate

Before implementation, review the proportions in top-down and cross-section diagrams. Adjust scale if the medium scenario looks too narrow, too tunnel-like, too empty, or too large to support the intended gameplay. Once a scale is accepted, write the approved values into the canon/decision section and only then create the blockout.

## Change note

Study B establishes proportional scenarios and a recommended medium-scale blockout for review. All numbers are provisional and must not be treated as approved world dimensions or copied into gameplay configuration.
