# District 14 — Visual Identity & Art Direction

## Core visual blend

District 14 combines the architectural language of Czechoslovak urban and residential construction from roughly 1950–1975 with retro-futurist space-habitat infrastructure. Cyberpunk is a secondary influence, used in details and systems rather than replacing the architecture.

## Architecture

Prioritize:
- prefabricated residential blocks and slab buildings;
- concrete, plaster, brick, ceramic cladding, and restrained painted panels;
- repetitive but believable window modules;
- balconies and loggias with real depth;
- stair cores, entrances, roof access and service volumes;
- ground-level shops and local services where suitable;
- rooftop antennas, utility equipment, vents, cable runs and maintenance details.

Buildings should not be generic cubes with decorative windows. Exterior dimensions, floor heights, window locations, stairs, interior layouts, roof geometry, and collision volumes must agree.

## Retro-futurist layer

Technology should appear designed for daily use and maintenance:
- transit guidance and signage;
- terminals, kiosks, display panels;
- aerial-vehicle infrastructure;
- communications equipment;
- utility conduits and power systems;
- small areas of restrained luminous color.

Avoid an indiscriminate neon treatment, glossy mega-towers, excessive holograms, or visual noise that erases the older architecture.

## Material and palette

The palette is still open for a formal art bible. The initial direction favors concrete and mineral neutrals, muted painted façade colors, dark metal, weathered industrial surfaces, and selective warm/cool technology accents. Exact palette, texture scale, aging and material rules need a dedicated reference pass.

## Composition and scale

- Main Street is the dominant visual axis.
- Building rows frame the street and help establish distance.
- The ring is a large-scale background/overhead structure, revealed with deliberate sightlines.
- The artificial atmosphere creates depth and softens far geometry.
- Vertical transport and aerial vehicles must respect façade, roof, and street scale.
- The world is limited to three streets but should not feel like a tiny disconnected set.

## Reference use

Reference images and image-generation outputs are visual inspiration only. They do not automatically define canon, exact building dimensions, construction methods, or asset specifications.
