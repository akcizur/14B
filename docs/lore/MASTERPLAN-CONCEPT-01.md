# District 14 — Masterplan Concept 01

> **Purpose:** Think through the spatial and narrative identity before adding more world geometry.
>
> **Status:** DESIGN PROPOSAL, not approved canon. This document records the recommended direction and the alternatives that still need a decision. No proposed dimensions in this document are implementation-ready.

## 1. Design problem

District 14 must read as a place with a coherent identity, not as an ordinary street map decorated with sci-fi effects. It has a small, intentionally limited street network, but should feel deep, large, and lived-in from street level and during vertical travel.

The central design challenge is to reconcile:
- a long, dominant Main Street;
- exactly three streets, without a conventional grid or many parallel roads;
- housing and urban frontage on both sides of Main Street;
- a protected artificial habitat in outer space;
- a monumental ring enclosure inspired by the user's Starship-ring concept;
- artificial atmosphere and controlled sky/illumination;
- vertical expansion and aerial traffic;
- a familiar Czechoslovak residential/urban character, especially 1950–1975, with technology that feels retro-futurist and integrated.

## 2. Recommended spatial concept

### Recommendation: an elongated, enclosed urban habitat

Treat District 14 as a long, enclosed habitat volume protected by a monumental ring-like structural system. For planning purposes, imagine an elongated capsule/stadium footprint with rounded ends rather than a perfect circle. This is a **shape study recommendation**, not a confirmed ring geometry.

This form supports a long Main Street and gives the player a sense of depth while allowing the enclosure to be visibly massive. It also avoids a circular street layout or the impression that the world is a small loop.

The city should occupy only a deliberate portion of the interior. The ring/enclosure remains a surrounding piece of infrastructure, not a road and not a continuous urban frontage.

### Why not a perfect circle by default?

A perfect circle would tend to encourage a circular road, radial streets, or a central plaza that compete with the intended linear city. It may still be considered if the visual or physical habitat concept later requires it, but it should not dictate the street topology.

### Why not a glass dome?

A transparent bubble risks making the city look like a miniature display or greenhouse. Prefer a monumental engineered enclosure whose interior is revealed selectively: structural ribs, service bands, pressure/thermal systems, large apertures or windows where justified, and occasional views into outer space. Exact construction and protection mechanisms are fictional and remain open.

## 3. The three-street topology

**CANON CONSTRAINT:** exactly three streets. No procedural street-grid generation and no unapproved fourth street.

### Street 01 — Main Street

- One long, dominant linear axis.
- Residential and mixed-use buildings frame both sides for substantial stretches.
- Strong, readable vanishing line and street rhythm.
- The route changes character gradually through districts or segments, not by random swaps.
- It should feel longer than the immediately loaded/playable area.
- It can contain small widening spaces, stops, crossings, frontage changes, parking pockets and service access, but these do not become additional streets.

### Street 02 — Secondary connector (proposed role)

Recommended as a shorter cross-street branching from Main Street at a selected node. It creates a local change of perspective, a different building grouping, and a useful connection without starting a grid. It may terminate in a courtyard, public space, habitat service area, or designed endpoint. The exact end is open.

### Street 03 — Secondary connector (proposed role)

Recommended as a second shorter cross-street at a different, non-aligned location along Main Street. It should have a different character and function from Street 02. It need not mirror Street 02. Its endpoint, connection and length are open.

### Preferred topology, not yet approved

Main Street remains continuous. Street 02 and Street 03 branch from it at separate, staggered locations and do **not** align into a parallel grid. This is the leading topology because it matches “only three streets” and “not too many parallel streets” while offering meaningful exploration. Alternative topology sketches should be compared before approval.

Avoid adding unnamed vehicle roads and later counting them as alleys or service streets. Pedestrian courtyards, entrance paths, building passages, stairs, roof paths, and non-road service access may exist, but must be clearly distinguished from streets in the masterplan.

## 4. Spatial hierarchy

Plan the world in this order, from ground to upper layers:

1. **Street space:** roadway, sidewalks, entrances, street furniture, crossings, planted/parking pockets, small public spaces.
2. **Building frontage:** continuous but varied rows, real entrance positions, mixed-use ground floors in selected areas.
3. **Building interior:** dimensionally consistent floorplates, rooms, stair core, shafts, window openings, roof and collision.
4. **Roof layer:** accessible only where deliberately designed; service equipment and rooftop circulation have believable clearances.
5. **Aerial mobility layer:** flycar paths and landing/boarding/parking nodes with defined envelopes and safe distances.
6. **Habitat infrastructure:** ring shell, structural spans, maintenance systems, environmental systems and lighting.
7. **Outer space:** visible only through intentional view corridors, windows/openings, or selected structural gaps.

These are spatial layers of the same world, not separate scenes or levels.

## 5. Ring and habitat: define what is visible before what is simulated

The ring is an established setting premise; its precise geometry and technical function are not. The “Starship Ring” name is a visual/concept reference, not a statement about real spacecraft engineering.

### Recommended visual behavior

- The ring should feel gigantic, structural, and distant.
- It should not appear as a small glowing hoop hovering over the map.
- It should be revealed selectively from street, roof, and air viewpoints.
- Scale cues should include structural depth, service zones, maintenance lights, and occlusion, used sparingly.
- Avoid perfectly repetitive visible segments at game-scale spacing.
- The enclosure should create a credible sense of protected volume and edge, not a glass terrarium.
- The city must remain the foreground subject; ring visibility is a contextual reveal.

### Open habitat questions

- Is the ring one continuous enclosure or an assembly of large structural arcs?
- What is the overall plan form and section?
- Which surfaces contain the pressure boundary?
- What systems maintain pressure and atmosphere?
- What produces the habitat's illumination and cycle?
- What is the gravitational model and orientation?
- Where are service, maintenance, emergency, and docking systems?
- Which views reveal outer space, and which are blocked by the enclosure?

Do not claim gravity, atmosphere retention, radiation protection, or other engineering functions are solved by the ring until the fictional system is designed.

## 6. Artificial atmosphere and sky

Artificial atmosphere is part of the habitat premise. The visual design should not simply place an Earth skybox inside a space structure.

### Recommended visual direction

- Layered atmospheric depth and distance haze.
- Sky illumination that feels designed and controlled, while still supporting a naturalistic city mood.
- A deliberate day/lighting cycle only after its mechanism and desired mood are chosen.
- Outer-space background elements visible only where enclosure geometry permits.
- Clouds, fog, weather, and wind are optional habitat effects, not assumed Earth weather.
- Ambient air movement can be suggested by sound, ventilation and environmental detail, without adding constant visual particles.

The city should be calm and inhabited rather than permanently dark, neon, foggy, or dystopian.

## 7. Urban character and architectural logic

### Dominant period

Use Czechoslovak urban and residential architecture from approximately 1950–1975 as the primary architectural language. Later retro-futurist additions should feel like successive upgrades, repairs, adaptations, and services layered onto existing buildings.

### Architectural families, not random building assets

Establish a limited family system before mass placement:
- low residential blocks;
- standard residential slabs;
- taller residential landmarks;
- mixed-use/ground-floor service buildings;
- selected civic or infrastructure buildings.

Each family must define its exterior envelope, floor heights, structural grid, stair core, interior logic, window rhythm, balconies/loggias, roof and collision. Variation should come from coherent module combinations and building history, not random façade noise.

### Street rhythm

Main Street should have long continuous frontage periods interrupted by deliberate events: entrances, corner/turning moments, shops, stops, service areas, small plazas, or distinctive blocks. Avoid a constant “same house repeated” effect and avoid random clutter. Use gradual neighborhood transitions.

## 8. Storytelling: ordinary life inside an extraordinary enclosure

The strongest narrative contrast is that the place is an artificial habitat in space, yet daily life can feel ordinary and Central-European: people enter apartment buildings, wait at stops, shop, maintain homes, walk dogs, repair infrastructure, and use familiar public spaces.

Do not reveal the whole premise through exposition. Environmental lore can emerge from:
- old building signs and street names;
- public notices and dated renovation panels;
- transit maps and habitat warnings;
- maintenance markings and service doors;
- architecture and adapted older equipment;
- glimpses of ring infrastructure and outer space;
- local routines and quiet audio cues.

The settlement's founding, population, political system, purpose, timeline, and major events remain **OPEN**. Avoid inventing definitive lore until it is explored and documented.

## 9. Mood and experience

Desired feeling:
- familiar, lived-in and slightly uncanny;
- quiet, spacious, isolated, and not constantly threatening;
- nostalgic architecture without becoming a museum;
- futuristic infrastructure that has become mundane;
- the scale of the enclosure revealed gradually;
- limited map geometry that feels expansive through perspective, verticality, occlusion and carefully controlled sightlines.

Avoid:
- generic cyberpunk megacity;
- neon on every surface;
- endless street grids;
- random procedural urban clutter;
- city streets visibly curling into a circular loop;
- a transparent dome miniature effect;
- repetitive buildings with no coherent façade/interior relationship;
- abrupt map edges or obvious duplicated street segments.

## 10. Masterplan decision sequence

Do not build more geometry until these design gates are addressed:

### Gate A — Habitat outline and section
Produce two or three conceptual plan/section options for the ring/enclosure. Compare how they support the long street, city volume, atmosphere, vertical space, and ring visibility. Do not commit dimensions yet.

### Gate B — Three-street topology
Draw a top-down plan of exactly three streets. Compare branch/intersection options. Show the Main Street's direction and length relative to the two secondary streets. No fourth road or parallel grid.

### Gate C — Main Street profile
Create one representative cross-section including roadway, sidewalks, façades, building setbacks, heights, roof line, aerial clearance and enclosure visibility. Dimensions must be measured or explicitly labeled provisional.

### Gate D — Building prototype
Use the actual reference building model. Validate exterior/interior alignment, window and glazing openings, stair geometry, entry-to-sidewalk connection, roof and collision. Do not furnish.

### Gate E — Vertical mobility
Only after building heights and habitat section are defined, decide the number of aerial layers, route envelopes, vehicle dimensions, vertical transitions and node types. Previous lane heights are prototypes, not canon.

### Gate F — Atmosphere and lighting
Define the sky/habitat lighting concept and street-level mood, then test with actual Main Street viewpoints.

### Gate G — Segment assembly and expansion
Only after the single segment is approved, decide segment length, streaming seams, repetition/variation logic, and how the long street remains visually continuous.

## 11. Recommended next deliverable

**District 14 Masterplan Study A** should be a design package, not more runtime code:

- three habitat plan/section sketches (descriptive and dimensioned only where grounded);
- three possible street topologies;
- recommended topology with rationale;
- Main Street cross-section;
- building height bands and street frontage rhythm;
- air/roof layer clearance envelope;
- ring visibility from ground, roof and air;
- unresolved decision list;
- acceptance criteria for a first playable segment.

No dimensions or visual choices become canon merely by appearing in a study. The user must review and approve them.
